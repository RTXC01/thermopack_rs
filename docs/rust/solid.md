# Melting and sublimation lines

This module implements the melting and sublimation line correlations of the
checked-in ThermoPack fork, and the Gibbs energy model of solid carbon
dioxide.

## Source scope

| Original routine | Rust |
| --- | --- |
| `src/solid_saturation.f90:melting_pressure_correlation` | `solid::melting_line` |
| `src/solid_saturation.f90:melting_pressure` | `solid::melting_pressure` |
| `src/solid_saturation.f90:sublimation_pressure_correlation` | `solid::sublimation_line` |
| `src/solid_saturation.f90:sublimation_pressure` | `solid::sublimation_pressure` |
| `src/solid_correlation_datadb.f90` | `solid_data` (generated) |
| `src/co2_gibbs.f90` | `solid_co2::SolidCo2` |

The parameter database is generated by `tools/rust_port/generate_solid_data.py`
from `src/solid_correlation_datadb.f90`: 23 melting curves and 16 sublimation
curves with their correlation label, triple point temperature, temperature
limits, reducing parameters, coefficients, exponents and reference.

The generated numbers use a round-trip format. The source tables carry up to
thirteen significant digits and two coefficients of the propane melting curve
differ in their last digit, which a fixed format would silently remove.

## Model

Both line families use the same reduced-temperature sum with a different
second term:

* the melting correlation uses $(T_\mathrm{red}-1)^{e}$,
* the sublimation correlation uses $(1-T_\mathrm{red})^{e}$,
* the first terms use $T_\mathrm{red}^{e}$, the third terms
  $\ln(T_\mathrm{red})^{e}$ and the remaining terms
  $(T_\mathrm{red}^{e}-1)$.

The last character of the correlation label selects the final expression:
`1` keeps the sum, `2` exponentiates it and `3` exponentiates the sum
divided by the reduced temperature. The `MP` melting label is a separate
two-branch correlation that the source splits at 22 K.

The temperature grid follows the source: the melting line starts at the triple
point and reaches the larger of the requested temperature and 1.1 times the
triple point temperature, limited by the correlation's maximum temperature. The
sublimation line starts at the smaller of the requested temperature and 0.9
times the triple point temperature, raised to the correlation's minimum
temperature, and reaches the triple point.

## Source limitations reproduced

* **Hydrogen sulfide.** The sublimation record of hydrogen sulfide carries the
  label `S2`, which has no fourth character. The original selects the final
  expression by that character and stops with an error. The native model
  reports the same error.
* **Missing correlations.** A component without a melting or sublimation
  record is not a line. The original returns an empty line, the native model
  reports an error.
* **Undefined values.** The xenon sublimation line at 50 K produces an
  undefined value in the original because the reduced temperature is not
  positive. The native model reproduces the undefined value.
* **Scaling to the equation of state.** The original can rescale the reducing
  pressure so that the triple point matches the bubble pressure of the active
  equation of state. That variant needs the mixture flash machinery and is
  not part of this port.

## Validation

`tools/rust_port/validate_solid_lines.py` compares the native model against
the exact-source library through a reference probe that installs the requested
component in the source globals, calls the source routine and restores the
globals afterwards.

* 156 cases: 23 melting and 16 sublimation components at four temperature
  limits each, with seven points per line.
* 152 cases match, covering the temperature grid and the pressure of every
  point. The tolerance is a relative 1e-10 with an absolute 1e-4 Pa, because
  some melting curves subtract two nearly equal numbers. The krypton line at
  its triple point, for example, cancels two terms of about 2346 Pa to a
  result of 0.72 Pa, which amplifies the rounding of the exponentiation by a
  factor of about three thousand. The thin link time optimization can also
  contract a multiplication and an addition into a fused operation in one
  binary and not in another, so the tolerance covers that rounding
  difference. The recorded validation run matched exactly.
* The 4 remaining cases are the hydrogen sulfide sublimation lines, where the
  original stops with an error and the native model reports the same error.

The committed fixtures are `tests/fixtures/solid_lines.csv` and
`tests/fixtures/solid_lines_status.csv`; the test is `tests/solid.rs`.

## Solid carbon dioxide

`src/co2_gibbs.f90` calculates the thermodynamic potentials of solid carbon
dioxide from a Gibbs energy expression of Jäger and Span, "Equation of State
for Solid Carbon Dioxide Based on the Gibbs Free Energy", J. Chem. Eng. Data
57 (2012) 590-597. The model provides the Gibbs energy with its first and
second temperature and pressure derivatives, and from them the specific
volume, entropy, enthalpy, internal energy, Helmholtz energy, heat capacity
and speed of sound.

The source module keeps two reference levels in module variables and shifts
them in `sco2init` so that the solid and the fluid phase of the equation of
state share the same reference. The native model does the same shift in
`SolidCo2::initialize` with the liquid entropy, the liquid Gibbs energy and
the triple point temperature and pressure. The model uses its own gas
constant, 8.314472 J/mol/K, which differs slightly from the value of the
equation of state library.

`tools/rust_port/validate_solid_co2.py` compares the native model against the
exact-source library. The source module keeps its reference levels, so the
reference probe shifts them once with the source routine and then evaluates
the states.

* 7 states below and above the triple point, with 13 quantities each: the
  Gibbs energy, the pressure derivative, the second pressure derivative, the
  temperature derivative, the second temperature derivative, the mixed
  derivative, the specific volume, the entropy, the enthalpy, the internal
  energy, the Helmholtz energy, the heat capacity and the speed of sound.
* All 7 states match with a relative tolerance of 1e-11 and quantity-specific
  absolute tolerances. The speed of sound is a square root of a small
  difference and needs the loosest of them.

The committed fixture is `tests/fixtures/solid_co2.csv`; the test is
`tests/solid_co2.rs`.

## Solid saturation line

`src/solid_saturation.f90` locates the state where a solid phase and the fluid
phase of the equation of state share the fugacity. The routine
`solidPointOnEnvelope` solves for the pressure of a given temperature or for the
temperature of a given pressure, with a Newton iteration in the logarithm of
both variables. The native model is in `solid_saturation`.

The solid contributes the logarithm of its fugacity coefficient

$$
\ln \phi^{\mathrm{solid}} = \frac{G^{\mathrm{solid}} - G^{\mathrm{ideal}}}{R T},
$$

and the fluid phase contributes the logarithm of its fugacity coefficient. The
residual of the iteration is the difference of the two. The original reduces
its residual to the same difference for a single component, because the
logarithm of the mole fraction of the solid component vanishes, and it uses
the same logarithmic variables in its Newton step. The native model evaluates
the derivatives of the residual by a centred difference of the same size, and
it applies the step in the logarithm of the pressure or of the temperature.

The reference levels of the solid model are part of the line. The original
`initDryIce` refines the triple point pressure of carbon dioxide with a bubble
pressure solve, takes the entropy and the Gibbs energy of the liquid at that
point and shifts the reference levels of the solid model onto them. The native
function `solid_co2_with_fluid_reference` performs the same shift with the
bubble pressure of the native equation of state, and both place the solid on
the liquid at the triple point of the original, 216.592 K and 513602.16 Pa.

`tools/rust_port/validate_solid_saturation.py` compares the native model
against the exact-source library through a reference probe that installs the
component in the source globals, initializes the source dry ice model and calls
the original routine.

* 6 cases: three temperature specifications at the triple point and above it,
  and three pressure specifications on the same line. The initial guess of the
  solved variable is close to the line, because the original is a local solver.
* All 6 cases match. The solved pressure agrees with a relative tolerance of
  1e-6 and the solved temperature with an absolute 1e-8 K and a relative 1e-9.
  The observed worst relative difference is 3.3e-8, in the pressure of the line
  at 216.6 K.

The committed fixture is `tests/fixtures/solid_saturation.csv`; the test is
`tests/solid_saturation.rs`.

## Remaining scope

* The solid saturation line is ported for a single component with the solid
  carbon dioxide model. The three-phase envelopes, the mixture flash with a
  solid phase and the hydrate model are not yet ported.
* The scaling of the reducing pressure to the equation of state is not
  ported.
* The solid phase is not yet connected to the mixture flash, so the formation
  of a solid phase is not part of the native flash.
